# Duka Smart Electronics: Business Insights

All figures are in KSh, use **Completed orders only**, and come from my query results.
"Today" is 30 September 2026, the latest order date in the data. Total completed revenue
is **KSh 24,155,371.70**, which reconciles across Q16, Q25, Q40 and Q45.

---

## 1. Accessories earn almost as much profit as Home Appliances on a quarter of the revenue

**Finding (Q16, Q33).** Mobile Phones, Computing and Electronics bring in KSh 19.05M of revenue
(74.7% of the total, about KSh 18.05M of it from these three), but their margins are only 9.92% to
10.61%. Accessories bring in just KSh 1.08M (4.5% of revenue) but earn a **42.39% margin**. Its profit
of KSh 456,746 is almost identical to Home Appliances' KSh 458,034, which sells KSh 4.02M. Overall
company margin is only 11.97%.

**Why it matters.** Every KSh 100 of accessories sold earns about KSh 42 in profit, against about
KSh 10 for phones. That is roughly four times more. The biggest sellers are working hard for thin
profit, and Electronics has the weakest margin at 9.92%.

**Recommendation.** Bundle accessories (cases, chargers, earphones) with every phone, laptop and TV
sale, and train staff to offer them at checkout. Review discounting on Electronics and Mobile Phones,
since small discount cuts there matter a lot at a 10% margin.

---

## 2. Nairobi produces 40% of revenue, and Machakos only 3%

**Finding (Q25).** Nairobi generated KSh 9.58M, which is **39.7%** of all revenue. Nairobi, Mombasa
(KSh 3.16M) and Nakuru (KSh 2.39M) together make up **62.7%**. At the other end, Machakos brought in
KSh 745,100 (3.1%) and Nyeri KSh 994,102 (4.1%). Nairobi earns about 13 times more than Machakos.

**Why it matters.** The business depends heavily on one city. A stock-out, delivery problem or new
competitor in Nairobi would hurt the whole company. The small towns may be under-served, but this
query alone can't say whether they simply have fewer customers or lower spending per customer.

**Recommendation.** Protect Nairobi first with reliable stock and fast delivery. Then compare revenue
per customer in each town (combine Q17 with Q25). If Machakos and Nyeri have enough customers but low
spending, test local promotions or M-Pesa-friendly offers there. If they simply have few customers,
focus on recruiting more.

---

## 3. VIP customers spend only 55% more than Retail, and Retail is still the volume engine

**Finding (Q27).** The average completed spend per purchasing customer is VIP KSh 253,252 (19
customers), Corporate KSh 197,959 (25) and Retail KSh 163,575 (88). VIP spends only about 1.55 times
the Retail average, and Corporate only 21% more. Retail is 67% of purchasing customers and
contributes roughly 60% of revenue (about KSh 14.4M), while VIP and Corporate each contribute about 20%.

**Why it matters.** The VIP label is not separating big spenders from normal ones very sharply.
The VIP average (KSh 253,252) only just clears the High Value threshold I used in Q45 (KSh 250,000),
which suggests many VIPs are not actually High Value customers. At the same time, Retail is where most
of the money comes from, so the segment deserves attention.

**Recommendation.** Review how customers are assigned to VIP and consider tiering by actual spend
rather than sign-up type. Keep VIP perks tied to spending levels, and run loyalty offers for Retail
customers close to the top of their group so they move up.

## 4. More than a third of purchasing customers have gone quiet

**Finding (Q34).** 47 of the 132 customers who have ever completed an order (**35.6%**) have not
completed a purchase in more than 90 days. 31 of them have been silent for over 180 days, and 7 for
more than a year, the longest being 625 days (last purchase 13 January 2025). Ten customers lapsed only
recently, 94 to 126 days ago.

**Why it matters.** Winning back an existing customer is cheaper than finding a new one, and these
customers have already shown they will buy. The longer they stay away, the less likely they are to
return. This measure only looks at completed orders, so a few of these customers may have a recent
pending order.

**Recommendation.** Start a win-back campaign this month, beginning with the ten customers who lapsed
94 to 126 days ago, using an SMS or WhatsApp message with a returning-customer offer. Add a rule that
flags any customer with no purchase for 60 days so the team can follow up earlier. Next, join Q34 with
total spending so the team contacts the highest-value lapsed customers first.

## 5. Revenue is growing fast, but December is a huge peak and January collapses

**Finding (Q40).** Revenue for January to September 2026 was KSh 12.83M, up **86.3%** on the same nine
months of 2025 (KSh 6.89M). December 2025 was the biggest month of that year at KSh 2.03M (59.7% above
November, and 17.9% of the year's revenue). Then January 2026 fell **84.09%** to KSh 322,433. That is
the lowest month in the whole dataset, and 25.9% below January 2025 even though the business was
growing strongly.

**Why it matters.** The business is clearly growing, but sales are very uneven. A festive-season
rush followed by a deep January slump strains cash flow and stock planning. Single-month growth
percentages (for example +136% in February 2026) look dramatic mostly because they start from a weak
month.

**Recommendation.** Plan stock and cash for a big December and a quiet January. Create a January
campaign (back-to-school or post-holiday offers, and corporate orders) to lift the weakest month, and
compare quarters or year-on-year figures when reporting growth, not only month-on-month.

## 6. 34 High Value customers produce 70% of revenue

**Finding (Q45).** I split customers using KSh 250,000 and KSh 50,000 as thresholds. The **34 High
Value customers** (23% of all 150) generated KSh 16.97M, which is **70.3%** of revenue, an average of
about KSh 499,000 each. The 58 Medium customers brought in KSh 6.43M (26.6%). The 58 Low Value
customers, which include 18 with no completed order, brought in only KSh 750,700 (3.1%), about KSh
12,900 each.

**Why it matters.** The business relies on a small group of customers. A High Value customer is worth
roughly 39 times a Low Value one, so losing even a few of them would be noticeable, while the Low
group costs about as much to serve as any other.

**Recommendation.** Give the High Value group personal attention: a named contact, early access to
stock and priority delivery. Check how many of them appear in the lapsed list from Insight 4 and
contact those first. Run targeted offers to move the best Medium customers up, and treat the 18
customers who never completed an order with a simple first-purchase offer.

*Method notes: revenue = quantity x unit_price x (1 - discount / 100) on Completed orders. Profit =
revenue - quantity x cost_price. Value band thresholds are explained in the comment under Q45.*
